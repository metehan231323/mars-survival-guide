🔬 JEZERO CRATER SCIENCE BRIEF

Jezero Crater is an ancient impact crater about 45 kilometers (28 miles) in diameter, located on Mars. It gained global prominence when it was chosen as the landing site for NASA's Mars 2020 mission, carrying the Perseverance rover and the Ingenuity helicopter.

1. Ancient Lake and River Delta

Billions of years ago, when Mars had a wet and potentially habitable climate, Jezero Crater held a large lake. An ancient river delta is clearly visible on the crater's rim, formed by sediment deposits carried by flowing water, much like deltas on Earth.

2. Search for Biosignatures & Organics

The fine-grained clay minerals and sediments at the ancient lake bed are ideal environments for preserving past microscopic life (microbial fossils). Perseverance is exploring here to search for signs of past life and collect rock samples for future return to Earth.

3. Rich Mineral Diversity

Data from NASA orbiters has shown the presence of carbonate and clay minerals within the crater. These minerals indicate water-rock interactions and are critical for tracking potential biological processes.
